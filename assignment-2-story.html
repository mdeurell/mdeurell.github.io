San Francisco's Deadly Drug Problem

San Francisco has shown a downward trend in several major crime types, but drug offenses stand out as the one category that has risen sharply. Looking at police reports back to 2003, the goal of this story is to understand how that pattern changed over time and what might explain it.

The selected focus data covers crime reports in San Francisco from 2003 to 2025, with an emphasis on the categories that were used throughout the plots in the analysis.

Drug-offense patterns

Out of the main crime types, drug offense is the one that increased most strongly over the last decade. One part of the story looks at how marijuana-related reports changed, while another focuses on harder drugs and the broader context around the fentanyl epidemic.

Data context

The underlying dataset combines the San Francisco police incident reports from 2003 onward and uses a harmonized focus-crime selection so that the categories can be compared consistently across years.

  • Crime categories are harmonized from the historical and current police report sources.
  • The analysis focuses on a reduced set of selected types that appear throughout the visuals.
  • The story highlights one strong long-term increase in drug offenses relative to other crime types.
